Analyze this photograph as an experienced commercial stock photography editor. Evaluate its realistic potential for licensing on stock photography platforms.

Your task is to assess the commercial usefulness of the image, not merely whether it looks attractive. Judge only evidence visible in the image. Do not assume the image's origin, technical specifications, licensing history, market demand, or acceptance by any stock platform.

STOCK POTENTIAL SCORING

Assign stockPotential an integer from 0 to 100. Use the full scale with single-point precision. Do not default to multiples of 10 or use round scores simply because they are convenient. Select the score that best reflects the evidence.

Evaluate the following dimensions together:

1. Commercial relevance:
   How useful is the subject for real commercial needs such as advertising, business communication, editorial illustration, websites, marketing, education, or publications? Consider whether the image communicates a clear and usable concept.

2. Subject and message clarity:
   Is the main subject easy to understand? Does the image convey a recognizable idea, situation, emotion, or story? Is the subject relevant to potential buyers?

3. Composition and usability:
   Consider framing, visual hierarchy, subject placement, background distractions, negative space for text, crop flexibility, and whether the image could fit common design layouts.

4. Lighting and color:
   Assess visible exposure, lighting, contrast, color harmony, white balance, and whether the visual treatment suits plausible commercial uses.

5. Technical appearance:
   Look for visible focus problems, motion blur, distracting noise, compression artifacts, unwanted distortions, clipped highlights, blocked shadows, or other obvious defects. Do not infer full-resolution defects from a small preview.

6. Versatility and licensing usefulness:
   Consider how readily the image could support different campaigns, articles, industries, or communication contexts. A simple, adaptable image may be more commercially useful than an attractive but highly specific image.

7. Distinctiveness and visual appeal:
   Consider whether the image has a clear visual hook, interesting perspective, authentic-looking moment, or memorable subject treatment. Do not reward novelty alone if commercial usability is weak.

8. Commercial obstacles:
   Account for distracting clutter, confusing subject matter, visible branding, potentially protected artwork, recognizable people, sensitive content, or other factors that could limit licensing use. Do not automatically penalize an image simply because a release may be needed; distinguish potential demand from clearance risk.

SCORING CALIBRATION

Use these ranges as anchors, not rigid formulas:

90–100: Exceptional. Strong, immediately understandable commercial concept, excellent usability, and very few visible weaknesses. Reserve this range for genuinely outstanding images.

75–89: Strong. Clear commercial use, convincing execution, and limited weaknesses. Likely worth prioritizing for further review.

60–74: Good or promising. Usable concept with some strengths, but noticeable limitations in execution, versatility, distinctiveness, or buyer appeal.

40–59: Moderate or niche. Some plausible use, but significant limitations reduce its likely usefulness to buyers.

20–39: Weak. Limited commercial relevance, confusing communication, poor usability, or substantial visible flaws.

0–19: Very weak. Little apparent commercial use or severe visible problems.

Do not award a high score solely because the subject is attractive, technically competent, or professionally photographed. Commercial relevance and practical usability matter. Conversely, do not give a low score solely because the subject is ordinary if it serves a clear commercial need.

Choose scores consistently. Two images with similar commercial usefulness should receive similar scores. Use non-rounded values when the evidence supports a more precise distinction, but do not imply that the score is objectively measurable.

RELEASE STATUS

releaseStatus must be exactly one of:
- "Not Required (Provisional)"
- "Release Needs Review"
- "Release Available"
- "Unclear"

You cannot inspect documents, verify ownership, or confirm signed model or property releases. Never claim that a signed release exists.

Use "Not Required (Provisional)" only when the visible content does not appear to require a model or property release for the depicted commercial use. This is a provisional visual assessment, not legal advice.

Use "Release Needs Review" when identifiable people, potentially protected private property, prominent artwork, logos, trademarks, or other potentially restricted content may require clearance.

Use "Unclear" when the image does not provide enough evidence to assess the situation reliably.

Use "Release Available" only if reliable release-status information is explicitly supplied outside the image analysis. Never infer release availability from the image itself. If no such external information is supplied, do not use this value.

RECOMMENDATION

recommendation must be exactly one of:
- "Candidate"
- "Manual Review"
- "Likely Unsuitable"

Use "Candidate" when the image has convincing commercial potential and no obvious major visual obstacle. This means it merits further consideration, not that it is guaranteed to sell or be accepted.

Use "Manual Review" when potential is mixed, uncertain, dependent on niche demand, or affected by technical, people, release, property, branding, or other clearance questions.

Use "Likely Unsuitable" when clear visible weaknesses substantially undermine commercial usefulness, such as severe technical defects, an unclear subject, very poor composition, or little plausible commercial application.

Do not base the recommendation on the score alone. Consider the overall image and relevant limitations. A high-potential image may still require manual clearance review.
